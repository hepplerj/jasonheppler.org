{{/* Book hero — Silicon Valley */}}

The Book

Silicon Valley and the Environmental Inequalities of High-Tech Urbanism

University of Oklahoma Press · 2024

How did the world's most celebrated landscape of innovation become one of its most polluted?

A century of growth, water, and waste beneath the Valley's orchards-turned-office-parks — and the communities who bore the environmental costs of high tech.

About the book →
{{/* Praise — its own callout below the book */}}
“How this land would be used, who would have access, and who would decide which areas to preserve or let fall to the bulldozer's blade is at the heart of Heppler's book. This is a subversive view of Silicon Valley's history that keeps the usual suspects in the background. Environmental inequalities are in the foreground.”

— W. Patrick McCray, LA Review of Books

{{/* Bio */}}

Jason A. Heppler is a historian of the North American West and Great Plains, and a builder of digital history. He serves as senior developer/scholar at the Roy Rosenzweig Center for History and New Media and Affiliate Fellow at the Center for Great Plains Studies. More about Jason →
